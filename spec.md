# SAVE YOUR NECK

### Working title — Bengali supernatural chaos card game

## 1. Game identity

A fast, funny, chaotic Bengali-themed survival card game for **3–8 players**, designed for family gatherings, friends, university hangouts and casual play.

Target game length: **~20 minutes**

Physical format: standard poker-size cards, portable, no board required. Tokens are optional and should only be added if they materially improve play.

Core fantasy:

> You are trying not to die horribly while everyone around you is stealing cards, lying about what they have, manipulating the deck and occasionally deciding whether to permanently remove a supernatural threat for everyone's benefit.

The tone should be **funny horror**, not serious horror.

Visual direction:

* Bengali folk-art / patachitra influence
* dark horror illustration
* vintage Bengali poster/pulp aesthetics
* deliberately exaggerated and comedic
* grotesque situations presented humorously rather than realistically

Language: primarily English rules/effects with Bengali creature names and Bengali cultural terminology.

Initial market: Bangladesh.

Target retail price: approximately **৳1,200**.

---

# 2. The fundamental game loop

Each player starts with **5 cards**.

On your turn:

1. Play any number of legal utility cards.
2. Or immediately draw.
3. The moment you draw a card, your turn normally ends.
4. If the card is a normal card, add it to your hand.
5. If the card is a Ghost, reveal it immediately.
6. Resolve the Ghost:

   * use its matching Protection;
   * use a General Protection;
   * negotiate a 1-for-1 protection trade;
   * permanently remove the Ghost using two matching Protection cards;
   * or die.
7. After the Ghost is resolved, the turn ends.

There is **no limit to the number of utility cards** you can play before drawing.

The central decision is therefore:

> "Do I keep manipulating the table, or do I risk drawing?"

---

# 3. Victory

There is only one winner.

**The last living player wins.**

There are no points.

There are no rounds that award partial victories.

There is no team mode in the base game.

---

# 4. Ghosts

Number of Ghosts:

> **Players − 1**

Therefore:

| Players | Ghosts |
| ------: | -----: |
|       3 |      2 |
|       4 |      3 |
|       5 |      4 |
|       6 |      5 |
|       7 |      6 |
|       8 |      7 |

This is one of the strongest structural decisions in the game because it naturally creates the final-player-standing condition.

Every Ghost has:

* 1 unique Ghost card type
* 2 identical matching Protection cards
* 1 horrifying/funny death
* 1 ridiculous way to survive it

The Ghost itself is **publicly visible whenever revealed**.

Ghosts are never placed into players' starting hands.

---

# 5. The seven Ghosts

The first seven Ghost concepts:

### 1. Petni

**Death:** "She steals your breath."

Matching protection:

**SAVE YOUR BREATH**

The artwork should exaggerate the classic wild-haired Petni into something simultaneously terrifying and ridiculous.

Petni is a documented Bengali folklore figure, although accounts vary considerably by region and tradition. Its association with unfulfilled desires and female ghosts gives plenty of room for a deliberately loose game adaptation.

---

### 2. Ghar Motkano Bhut

**Death:** "It twists your neck."

Matching protection:

**CHIROPRACTOR**

This is deliberately absurd.

The Ghost illustration should look like it is about to perform an impossible neck twist while the Chiropractor card depicts someone completely unfazed by the situation.

---

### 3. Kolla Kata Bhut

**Death:** "It tears your head clean off."

Matching protection:

**GOOD WITCH**

Effect text can simply read:

> "She stitches your head back on."

This is intentionally cartoonish.

---

### 4. Nishi

**Death:** "It calls your name. You answer. You disappear."

Matching protection:

**DON'T ANSWER**

The card could depict someone staring at their phone while an enormous ghost is standing behind them.

Nishi's association with calling people by a familiar voice and luring them away is well established in Bengali ghost-story traditions.

---

### 5. Mechho Bhut

**Death:** "It takes your fish. Then it takes you."

Matching protection:

**SAVE YOUR FISH**

This one should be deliberately comedic.

A giant fish could be depicted as the actual defensive object.

Mechho Bhoot is strongly associated with fish and fishermen in Bengali folklore.

---

### 6. Skondhokata

**Death:** "It mistakes your head for its missing one."

Matching protection:

**SAVE YOUR HEAD**

Visually, the Ghost is desperately searching for its own missing head while holding somebody else's.

Skondhokata is a documented Bengali headless-ghost figure, commonly associated with violent death and wandering in search of its missing head.

---

### 7. Mamdo Bhut

**Death:** "It drags you into the grave."

Matching protection:

**SAVE YOUR SOUL**

The protection card can depict an extremely annoyed person being pulled backward by an obviously theatrical ghost.

Mamdo Bhoot is part of Bengali ghost folklore and is commonly associated with Muslim ghosts.

---

# 6. Protection cards

This is where the mathematical design matters.

For every Ghost:

> **2 identical specific Protection cards**

So the 8-player game contains:

**7 Ghosts + 14 specific Protection cards**

But there are NOT 14 unique Protection designs.

There are:

> **7 Protection types × 2 copies each = 14 physical cards**

This distinction is necessary because your permanent-removal rule requires **two identical protection cards**.

Example:

Petni
→ Save Your Breath ×2

Ghar Motkano Bhut
→ Chiropractor ×2

etc.

---

# 7. General Protection

The game also contains:

> **Players − 1 General Protection cards**

At 8 players:

**7 General Protection cards**

These all have the same mechanical effect but different names and illustrations.

Possible cards:

* Ojha's Jhar
* Gunin's Tabiz
* Bhoot Bhaga Mantra
* Iron Charm
* Mustard & Salt
* Seven-Thread Raksha
* Protective Amulet

The exact names can be refined during art/lore development.

These cards:

> **Protect against ANY Ghost.**

However:

> **They can never permanently remove a Ghost.**

They only save the player and allow the Ghost to return to the deck.

Ojha, Gunin and Kabiraj are useful thematic vocabulary because traditional healing and spirit-related practices involving these figures are documented in Bangladesh and Bengal.

---

# 8. The critical mathematical setup rule

Pure random dealing creates a problem.

If every card were shuffled and dealt completely randomly, a player could theoretically receive five utility cards and have **zero protection cards**.

Because protection trading is restricted to protection-for-protection, that player could then be unable to bargain for survival.

That violates the intended design.

Therefore:

### Initial deal

Before dealing:

1. Separate all Ghost cards.
2. Separate all Protection cards.
3. Give **each player exactly one Protection card**.
4. Specifically distribute the initial Protection cards so that:

   * every player receives one Protection;
   * every Ghost has at least one matching Protection card somewhere in the table's hands.
5. Deal each player four additional cards from the remaining non-Ghost cards.
6. Shuffle all remaining cards together with all Ghosts.
7. This becomes the draw deck.

This produces a controlled starting state while keeping everything after the first draw chaotic.

For an 8-player game:

* 7 Ghosts
* 14 specific Protection
* 7 General Protection
* 24 Utility
* 40 cards dealt
* 12 cards initially in the draw pile

Every Ghost has at least one accessible counter in someone's hand.

That gives the game a **guaranteed cooperative survival route**, rather than merely a high probability of survival.

---

# 9. Ghost resolution

When you draw a Ghost:

### Step 1 — Reveal it.

Everyone sees which Ghost attacked you.

### Step 2 — Check your hand.

If you possess the matching Protection, you may use it immediately.

### Step 3 — Ask the table.

If you don't have it, you announce the required Protection.

Example:

> "I need Save Your Breath."

Players may respond.

### Step 4 — Trade

Trading is:

**strictly 1-for-1 Protection card for Protection card.**

The other card does not have to correspond to the same Ghost.

Example:

You have:

> Save Your Eyes

Another player has:

> Save Your Breath

You can trade:

> Save Your Eyes → Save Your Breath

You then survive Petni.

### Important:

The owner can simply refuse.

They cannot be forced to trade.

The attacked player may keep asking and making offers to different players.

There is no limit to the number of offers.

---

# 10. Lying

Negotiation is intentionally unrestricted by truthfulness.

Players may lie.

Examples:

> "I don't have Save Your Breath."

> "That's my only Protection."

> "I have nothing useful."

All of these can be lies.

Cards themselves remain hidden unless a rule specifically reveals them.

This is an important part of the social game.

---

# 11. No future promises

Trades must be immediate.

You cannot make binding future deals.

Invalid:

> "Give me Save Your Breath and I'll give you my good card next round."

Valid:

> "I'll give you Save Your Eyes right now for Save Your Breath."

This prevents the game from becoming a political negotiation game and keeps it fast.

---

# 12. Saving a Ghost

After obtaining the required protection, the attacked player has a choice.

### Option A — Survive

Discard the Protection card permanently.

Take the Ghost and place it **anywhere in the draw deck**.

The player may choose the position.

They may reveal where they put it.

They may keep the placement secret.

Turn ends.

---

### Option B — Permanently remove the Ghost

If the player has **two copies of the exact matching Protection card**, they may sacrifice both.

Example:

Petni attacks.

Player has:

> Save Your Breath
> Save Your Breath

They may discard:

> Petni + Save Your Breath + Save Your Breath

All three are permanently removed from the game.

Petni can never kill anybody again.

This is the game's major collective-risk mechanic.

---

# 13. The deception layer

The permanent-removal decision should be psychologically important.

Suppose Petni attacks.

You have:

> Save Your Breath ×2

You could permanently eliminate Petni.

But perhaps you want other players to think Petni is still somewhere in the deck.

So you can simply use one Protection and secretly bury Petni somewhere.

Nobody knows whether:

* you had one counter;
* you had two;
* you traded for one;
* you deliberately kept Petni alive.

This is where the game gets its social deception.

---

# 14. General Protection

If you use a General Protection:

> discard the General Protection permanently.

Then:

> put the Ghost anywhere in the deck.

The Ghost survives.

A General Protection **cannot** participate in permanent Ghost removal.

Permanent removal requires:

> Ghost + two identical matching Protection cards.

---

# 15. Death

If the attacked player cannot obtain the required protection:

**They die.**

Their entire hand is permanently discarded.

The Ghost that killed them is **not discarded**.

Before leaving the game, the dead player places that Ghost anywhere in the draw deck.

They may choose the position.

The dead player's other cards are gone permanently.

This means death makes the remaining game progressively more dangerous because the available Protection supply shrinks.

---

# 16. Normal cards

If you draw a non-Ghost card:

> Add it to your hand.

Your turn ends immediately.

No effect occurs simply because the card was drawn.

---

# 17. Utility cards

Utility cards are the chaos engine.

They can be played freely during your pre-draw phase.

There is:

> **No maximum number of utility cards you may play.**

Therefore:

> Shakchunni → Robber → Shuffle → Skip

could all potentially happen during the same turn, assuming their conditions are satisfied.

Once you draw, your turn ends.

---

# 18. Initial 24-card Utility pool

The 8-player version contains **24 physical Utility cards**.

### Core utilities — 3–4 players

16 cards:

| Utility    | Copies | Function                          |
| ---------- | -----: | --------------------------------- |
| Shakchunni |      2 | Randomly steal a card             |
| Robber     |      2 | Everyone discards one card        |
| Shuffle    |      2 | Shuffle draw deck                 |
| Skip       |      2 | End your turn without drawing     |
| Nope       |      2 | Cancel a utility                  |
| Chor       |      2 | Randomly steal from chosen player |
| Peek       |      2 | Inspect top cards                 |
| Reverse    |      2 | Reverse turn direction            |

### Advanced utilities — 5–6 players

Add 4 cards:

| Utility | Copies | Function                       |
| ------- | -----: | ------------------------------ |
| Attack  |      2 | Force target to take two draws |
| Kabiraj |      2 | Information/reordering utility |

Total:

**20 Utility cards**

### Full chaos — 7–8 players

Add 4 cards:

| Utility     | Copies | Function               |
| ----------- | -----: | ---------------------- |
| Ojha        |      2 | Two-copy combo         |
| Bhoot-Bodol |      2 | Hand/card manipulation |

Total:

**24 Utility cards**

The exact names can be refined after playtesting.

---

# 19. Shakchunni

Shakchunni is one of the signature mechanics and should remain distinctive.

When played:

### Target calculation

Count the number of cards currently in **your own hand**.

Then count that many seats around the table.

Skip yourself every time the count loops around.

The player reached becomes the target.

Example:

You have 4 cards.

Starting with the next player:

1 → Player B
2 → Player C
3 → Player D
4 → Player A

So Player A becomes the target.

If the count exceeds the number of other players, continue cycling.

---

### Stealing

The target holds their hand face-down.

The Shakchunni player selects one card blindly.

They **do not see the hand first**.

The stolen card enters the Shakchunni player's hand.

It can be used normally.

If it is a utility card, it can be played later during that same turn.

If it is a Protection card, it remains usable only when its matching Ghost appears.

The stolen card is treated exactly as though it had originally belonged to the thief.

---

# 20. Robber

Robber:

> **Every player discards exactly one card.**

This includes the player who played Robber.

Each player chooses their own card.

The cards are permanently removed.

Cards remain hidden.

Ghosts are the exception: if a Ghost somehow needs to be publicly handled, it remains visible.

Robber therefore has the funny strategic property of:

> "I am hurting myself, but I'm hurting everyone else too."

---

# 21. Shuffle

Shuffle:

> Shuffle the entire draw deck.

No discarded cards return.

No hands are affected.

This becomes particularly powerful after someone secretly places a Ghost somewhere.

---

# 22. Skip

Skip:

> End your turn immediately.

You do **not** draw.

This is one of the simplest and most important survival cards.

---

# 23. Nope

Nope is a reaction card.

It may be played when another player plays a Utility card.

The Utility card is cancelled and permanently discarded.

A Nope can itself be Noped.

This creates the classic:

> Nope → Nope → Nope

chaos.

Nope does not cancel:

* drawing;
* Ghost attacks;
* Protection;
* death.

It only cancels Utility effects.

---

# 24. Reverse

Reverse changes the direction of play.

Default:

> clockwise

Reverse:

> counter-clockwise

Another Reverse changes it again.

The direction remains changed until another Reverse is played.

---

# 25. Peek

Peek allows the player to secretly inspect the top **3 cards** of the draw deck and return them in any order.

They do not show the cards.

This creates information without completely eliminating uncertainty.

---

# 26. Chor

Chor is a straightforward theft utility.

Choose another player.

They hold their hand face-down.

Randomly take one card.

You may use it normally.

Unlike Protection trading, theft has no requirement that the stolen card be a particular type.

---

# 27. Attack

Attack is inspired by Exploding Kittens but adapted to this game's turn structure.

Choose another player.

That player owes **two consecutive draws**.

After their first draw:

* normal card → immediately perform the second draw;
* Ghost → resolve the Ghost first;
* if they die → the second draw disappears with them;
* if they survive → they must perform their second draw.

This makes Attack particularly dangerous because a player cannot simply draw one safe card and assume they're finished.

---

# 28. Kabiraj

Kabiraj introduces the first stacking mechanic.

One Kabiraj:

> Look at the top card of the draw deck.

Two Kabiraj played together:

> Look at the top 3 cards and rearrange them in any order.

After use, the Kabiraj cards are permanently discarded.

This creates a meaningful reason to hold a useless-looking single copy.

---

# 29. Ojha

Ojha is the first true **pair-required utility**.

One Ojha:

> Does nothing.

Two Ojhas:

> Choose another player. If they possess a Protection card, they must randomly discard one Protection card.

Both Ojha cards are permanently discarded.

The target does not reveal which Protection card they lost.

This creates a nasty strategic decision:

> Do I waste space holding one useless Ojha, hoping to acquire another?

The answer can change after a Shakchunni or Chor steals one.

---

# 30. Bhoot-Bodol

Bhoot-Bodol is a later-game chaos card.

One copy:

> Exchange one random card from your hand with one random card from another player's hand.

Two copies:

> Choose two other players. Randomly exchange one card between each of them.

Both cards are discarded after use.

This is intentionally chaotic because the players don't know what they're giving away.

---

# 31. Utility stacking rules

There are three types of utility cards.

### Solo

One copy works normally.

Example:

> Shakchunni

### Pair required

One copy is useless.

Two identical copies played together activate the effect.

Example:

> Ojha ×2

### Enhanced pair

One copy has a weak effect.

Two copies have a stronger effect.

Example:

> Kabiraj ×1 → peek at top card
> Kabiraj ×2 → inspect/reorder top 3

Both copies are discarded after activation.

A card's stacking requirement is printed clearly on the card.

---

# 32. Card ownership

Once a card enters your hand, it behaves exactly like your own card.

This applies even if you obtained it through:

* Shakchunni
* Chor
* Bhoot-Bodol
* another stealing effect

There is no concept of a "stolen card."

If you steal someone's Save Your Breath, it is now your Save Your Breath.

---

# 33. Information rules

Hands:

> Secret.

Utility cards played:

> Public.

Protection trades:

> Public.

Ghosts:

> Public.

Discarded Utility/Protection cards:

> Hidden.

Discarded Ghosts:

> Public.

Players cannot inspect another player's hand.

Players may lie about what they possess.

This creates three layers of information:

**Known:** Ghosts and played cards.

**Partially inferable:** Protection supply and previous trades.

**Unknown:** Current hands and secret Ghost placement.

---

# 34. Starting player

Two acceptable options:

### Random

Draw a card / roll / otherwise randomly determine the first player.

### Thematic

> Whoever tells the worst/scariest ghost story goes first.

For the commercial rules, I would make the default:

> **The person who most recently heard a ghost story goes first.**

If nobody can agree, choose randomly.

This gives the game personality without introducing an unnecessary component.

---

The game should explicitly provide different setups rather than pretending one deck configuration works equally well at every player count.

|       3 |      2 |                   4 |                  2 |      16 |          24 |
|       4 |      3 |                   6 |                  3 |      16 |          28 |

|       7 |      6 |                  12 |                  6 |      24 |          48 |
|       8 |      7 |                  14 |                  7 |      24 |          52 |

Each player starts with 5 cards.

Ghosts are excluded from the initial deal and shuffled into the remaining deck afterward.

| Players | Initial draw pile |

The game does not need a giant draw deck because Ghosts are repeatedly recycled into the deck after survival or death.

# 36. Why the math works

For an 8-player game:

* 7 Ghosts
* 14 matching Protection
* 7 General Protection
* 24 Utility
* 52 total cards

Each player receives 5 cards:

> 40 cards dealt.

Ghosts are held out of the initial deal.

The remaining:

> 12 cards

become the draw deck.

The key property is not probability.

The key property is the **controlled Protection distribution**.

Before the four random cards are dealt:

> Every player gets one Protection.

And:

> At least one matching Protection for every Ghost is deliberately distributed among the players.

Therefore, if Player A draws Petni:

* Player A may already have Save Your Breath.
* Or Player A may have a General Protection.
* Or Player A can offer their Protection card to someone holding Save Your Breath.
* If that person cooperates, Petni can be survived.

Thus:

> **The first Ghost encounter has a guaranteed cooperative survival route.**

That is much better game design than trying to achieve the same property through a huge deck and probability.

---

# 37. The important probability

After the controlled protection deal, the remaining four cards per player are random.

This means the game still has randomness without gambling the entire first encounter on the shuffle.

For example, in the 8-player configuration there are 45 non-Ghost cards.

The two specific Protection cards for any particular Ghost have a roughly **99% chance of having at least one copy among the 40 initial player cards** under an ordinary random deal of the remaining cards.

But the setup rule makes this stronger:

> We don't need to rely on that probability.

One matching counter is deliberately seeded into someone's starting hand.

That is the mathematical backbone of the game.

---

# 38. What makes the game interesting

There are four competing resources.

### Life

You have exactly one.

### Protection

Protects you from death but disappears permanently.

### Information

Knowing where a Ghost might be is valuable.

### Manipulation

Utility cards allow you to change who has what and what happens next.

This produces a useful strategic tension:

> Removing a Ghost makes the game safer.

But:

> Removing a Ghost also requires destroying two valuable Protection cards.

Therefore the rational decision is not always to remove the Ghost.

Sometimes keeping it alive is better.

---

# 39. The central psychological decision

When you have two matching Protection cards:

> "Do I eliminate this Ghost permanently?"

versus:

> "Do I save myself cheaply and secretly put it somewhere horrible?"

That should become one of the game's signature moments.

The player can even bluff:

> "Sorry guys, I only had one."

when they actually had two.

Nobody can verify it.

---

# 40. Death escalation

Every death removes the dead player's entire hand.

Therefore:

* Protection disappears.
* Utility disappears.
* Useful information disappears.
* The remaining Protection supply becomes smaller.

Meanwhile Ghosts continue circulating.

This means the game should become increasingly dangerous as players die.

That naturally produces a late-game acceleration.

---

# 41. The intended 20-minute rhythm

The game should roughly feel like:

### Early game

Everyone has lots of cards.

Players establish their Protection resources.

People lie.

People steal.

Ghosts are mostly survivable.

### Mid game

Protection begins disappearing.

Players start remembering who probably has what.

Ghosts get deliberately buried.

Players begin making political-looking bargains even though there are no binding deals.

### Late game

Very few Protection cards remain.

Ghost placement becomes extremely important.

A single Shuffle can completely destroy everyone's assumptions.

A single stolen Protection can change the winner.

A Ghost that everyone thought was safely buried suddenly appears.

### End game

Two or three players remain.

Everyone knows the remaining Protection situation.

Nobody trusts anybody.

Someone draws.

Everyone shuts up.

---

# 42. What the physical prototype should test

Do NOT initially test artwork quality.

Test these five things:

1. **Does drawing a Ghost feel exciting?**
2. **Is trading Protection actually interesting?**
3. **Is permanent Ghost removal worth sacrificing two Protection cards?**
4. **Are Utility cards strong enough to make pre-draw play worthwhile?**
5. **Does the game actually finish in ~20 minutes?**

The most important metric is not whether players understand the rules.

It is whether players voluntarily say:

> "Again."

# 44. Design principle for the artwork

The artwork should not make the game look like generic Western Halloween.

It should look unmistakably Bengali.

Think:

* village ponds
* banyan trees
* rickety bridges
* railway tracks
* old Dhaka-style houses
* rural kitchens
* fishing nets
* hurricane lamps
* shankha/pola motifs where culturally appropriate
* Bengali typography accents
* old pulp-horror poster composition
* exaggerated facial expressions
* absurd physical injuries

A Petni shouldn't just look like "Asian ghost girl #17."

A Mechho Bhut should look like an aggressively Bengali fish-obsessed menace.

A Chiropractor card should be almost comically mundane compared with the supernatural thing it is fixing.

That contrast is part of the game's identity.

---

# 45. Current biggest balance risks

These are the things I would expect to break during playtesting.

### Risk 1 — Protection becomes too scarce

If people die too quickly after the first few rounds, increase General Protection.

### Risk 2 — Protection becomes too abundant

If nobody dies for 20–30 minutes, reduce General Protection or increase Utility theft.

### Risk 3 — Skip becomes too powerful

If everyone simply skips dangerous draws, limit the number of Skip cards.

### Risk 4 — Shakchunni becomes oppressive

If the card repeatedly steals the only useful Protection from a player, reduce its copies or give it a restriction.

### Risk 5 — Permanent removal becomes obviously optimal

If everyone always sacrifices two Protection cards immediately, the decision has no strategic tension.

### Risk 6 — Permanent removal is never used

Then the two-copy requirement may be too expensive.

### Risk 7 — Attack dominates

If Attack causes too many unavoidable deaths, make the second draw optional or allow a defensive utility.

### Risk 8 — Utility cards overshadow Ghosts

The game should always feel like a **Ghost survival game**, not an ordinary take-that card game with Ghost artwork.

---

# 46. The most important design rule

The game should never become:

> "I drew the wrong card, so I lose."

It should instead become:

> "I drew the wrong card. Now I need to convince someone who has exactly what I need to give it to me."

That is the heart of the game.

The Ghost creates the threat.

The Protection cards create the economy.

The Utilities create chaos.

The hidden hands create deception.

The permanent Ghost-removal rule creates long-term strategy.

And the 20-minute runtime keeps all of it from becoming exhausting.

---

# 47. Prototype version

The first physical prototype should therefore be:

**8-player / 52-card full deck**

even though the game supports 3–8.

Why?

Because the full version contains every mechanic.

Once that works, the 3–7 player versions are simply curated subsets according to the table above.

Do not design six completely independent games.

Design **one game with six controlled configurations**.

---

# 48. What should happen next

The next design task should NOT be more brainstorming.

It should be a **mathematical simulation + rules audit**.

Specifically, we should simulate thousands of games using the current rules and measure:

* average game length
* average number of deaths
* probability of first-round death
* Ghost encounter frequency
* Protection consumption
* General Protection usage
* permanent Ghost removal frequency
* Utility-card usage
* Shakchunni impact
* Attack impact
* expected hand sizes
* probability of deck exhaustion
* how often a player reaches a state with no Protection
* how often the final two players are effectively decided before the last draw

Then we adjust the card counts and effects.

That is the point where this stops being a collection of cool ideas and becomes an actual game design.
